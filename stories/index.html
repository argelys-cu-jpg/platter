Essay

The freezer is not a personality. It is a tool.

By Marco Diaz•Essay•11 min

Somewhere between the pandemic and the Instagram fridge tours, the freezer stopped being a piece of kitchen equipment and became a lifestyle. A moral posture. A signal of preparedness. Which is, if you spend any time cooking, faintly absurd.

The freezer, at its best, is a tool for buying yourself one specific thing: a good dinner on a night you did not plan for. It is not a philosophy. It is a drawer.

Once you stop asking the freezer to be more than it is, you get much better at using it. You start freezing what actually reheats — stews with fat in them, cooked grains in flat packs, half-portions of stock. You stop freezing the things you were freezing to feel productive.
